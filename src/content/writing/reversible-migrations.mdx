There's a quiet confidence in a forward-only migration. It says: I know exactly what this
change does and I will never need to undo it. Occasionally that's true. Usually it's a bet
you're making without noticing.

## The road back

My rule of thumb: write every schema change so it can be reverted without calling a meeting.
Not because you'll always revert, but because the discipline of designing the reverse path
surfaces the risk you were about to take.

If you can't cleanly reverse a change, that's not a reason to skip the reverse — it's a signal
the change is bigger than it looked.

## Expand, migrate, contract

The pattern that makes this practical is boring and old:

1. **Expand** — add the new shape alongside the old.
2. **Migrate** — move reads and writes over, gradually.
3. **Contract** — remove the old shape once nothing depends on it.

Each step is independently reversible. You're never more than one small, understood move away
from where you just were. That's what makes moving fast actually safe.
