There's a fixed amount of novelty a team can absorb before things get fragile. I think of it
as a budget. The question is never whether to spend it, but where.

## Two places to spend

You can spend novelty on **infrastructure** — a new database paradigm, a bleeding-edge
runtime, an exotic deployment model. Or you can spend it on **product** — an interaction
nobody has quite done, a workflow that reshapes how people work.

Product novelty is usually where the value is. It's the reason anyone cares. Infrastructure
novelty is mostly invisible to the people you're building for; it only becomes visible when
it fails.

## The default

So my default is boring infrastructure, interesting product. Proven storage, unremarkable
deploys, a stack a new hire can reason about on day one — paired with a product surface that
takes real risks.

This isn't conservatism for its own sake. It's about concentrating your risk where it can
pay off, and removing it everywhere it can only cost you.
